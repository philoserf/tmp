## Finding

Across all 53 instances of `fully` in Drafts/ as of 2026-05-08, zero are bare-intensifier filler. The author uses `fully` precisely:

- Distinguishing partial from complete: "never fully closes the gap", "no model fully captures"
- Parallel structures: "neither firmly in the past nor fully in the future"
- Philosophical precision: "belonging fully to none of them" (Derrida's "belongs to neither")

## Implication

When running filler-word passes on this corpus (e.g., issue #58), don't budget time for `fully`. Other words in the same Orwell-cluster (`simply`, `simultaneously`, `genuinely`, `fundamental`) have meaningful cut rates (15–25%); `fully` does not.

## Adjacent finding (corpus-wide)

Most "filler words" in this vault are load-bearing because the author leans on three rhetorical moves repeatedly:

1. Paired contrast: "not simply X but Y"
2. Modal-paired: "cannot simply X"
3. Real-vs-apparent: "genuinely X vs claimed X"

A first-pass cleanup that doesn't recognize these moves will flatten the prose.
