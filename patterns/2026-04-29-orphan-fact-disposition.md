When integrating salvage from culled or unofficial sources into a canonical-prose project, every orphan fact gets one of three fates:

1. **Canonical confirmation → integrate with canonical citation.** The orphan claim turns out to be canonical; cite the canonical source instead of the orphan.
2. **Canonical contradiction → drop.** The orphan claim conflicts with what canon says. Don't preserve.
3. **Flavorful internal inconsistency → preserve and document.** The contradiction has narrative interest (e.g., a title shifting between informal and formal usage at a polity's founding). Document the choice in the canon-contradiction Note section.

## Verification rule

Before disposition: check the orphan claim against the canonical subsector sourcebook and theme sourcebooks. Don't integrate without verification — orphan claims sometimes invent locations, names, or dates that conflict with canonical material.

## When to invoke (c) over (a)

User judgment call. The default in mechanical reconciliation is (a) or (b). Option (c) is appropriate when the inconsistency reads as period-appropriate, captures narrative texture, or reflects how the source material itself frames the founding moment differently from later accounts.
