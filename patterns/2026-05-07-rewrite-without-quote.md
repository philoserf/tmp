## Rule

When a piece is structured as commentary on a quote but the quote needs to be removed (missing attribution, no longer relevant, distracting from the argument), rewrite around the underlying claim instead of trying to preserve the quote-walkthrough scaffolding.

## Why

Quote-anchored pieces in the philoserf/notes corpus often follow this shape: open with a quote, then walk through its phrases or components paragraph by paragraph. When the quote is removed, the scaffolding has nothing to scaffold. Mechanical removal leaves a hollow piece that feels like a tour without a building.

The cleanest fix is to identify the underlying claim the quote was pointing at and rebuild the piece around that claim, using the freed-up paragraphs to add concrete texture instead of phrase-by-phrase analysis.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the underlying claim.** What is the piece actually arguing? It'"'"'s usually a single observation that the quote happens to capture.
2. **Lead with the claim, not the quote.** First sentence states the observation directly.
3. **Replace quote-component scaffolding with concrete + image.** Where the original walked through "phrase 1 means... phrase 2 means... phrase 3 means...", instead use one concrete example and one anchoring image to ground the abstract claim.
4. **Preserve any list-style content.** If the original quote-walkthrough included a vivid list (e.g., "rumination, fantasy, worry, rehearsal"), keep it — those are doing real work.
5. **Cut the summary closing.** Quote-walkthrough pieces often end by restating the quote'"'"'s meaning; without the quote, this restatement is pointless.

## Example

In Privacy of Mental Habits (philoserf/notes, 2026-05-07):

- Original: 6 paragraphs, opened with `"…or whatever else we are wont to do in the privacy of our minds"`, walked through "wont to do," "privacy of our minds," and "whatever else" as separate paragraphs.
- Rewrite: 4 paragraphs, opened with `Most of mental life happens unobserved`, added concrete example (`replaying a conversation from three years ago`) and image (`The named thoughts are spotlit; the rest is the room`), kept the operations list (rumination, fantasy, worry, etc.), cut the summary close.

Result: stand-alone observation, no longer commentary on someone else'"'"'s phrase.
