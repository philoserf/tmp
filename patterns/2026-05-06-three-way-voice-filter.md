## Pattern

When auditing the Drafts/ folder against the rule "Drafts = original prose only," there are three valid outputs, not two:

1. **Original prose stays in Drafts** — the user's voice, in active development.
2. **Useful synthesis moves to Reference** — research summaries, explanatory notes, curated material that earns keeping but isn't the user's authorial voice.
3. **Neither-of-those gets deleted** — notes that fail both filters get cut outright, not parked.

## Why

A two-output filter (move-or-stay) accumulates dead weight; the third output (delete) is what makes the editorial pass actually shrink the vault. Observed empirically on 2026-05-02: six notes moved Drafts→Reference, two deleted outright (`Drafts/Relational Physics Framework.md` 338 lines, `Drafts/Seed Oil Misinformation Defense.md` 215 lines).

## Companion editorial axes

This rule is one of three editorial conventions that crystallized across W17/W18 in philoserf/notes:

1. **Title is the noun, not the sentence** — titles should be claims or noun phrases, not "Understanding the…" or genre suffixes.
2. **Drafts = original prose only** — the rule above; three-way filter.
3. **Scope-narrow rewrite** — when a Reference note overclaims influence, identify the one or two real channels and explicitly disclaim the rest. Form: claim the smaller honest version, name what's commonly inflated, end with "that is enough; it does not need to be inflated into …" Exemplar: 4/29 rewrite of [[Celtic Influence on Western Culture]].

All three are short editorial filters that turn many small decisions fast — the same shape as the "in-world POV voice discipline" used on the Clement Sector setting.

## Application heuristic

When approaching a folder audit, name the filter binary first, then ask "what's the third output?" — usually deletion or relocation outside the audited folder.
